# 🔍 Artificial Intelligence in Digital Forensics

**Applications, challenges and explainability in the analysis of digital evidence**

A literature-review article written for the Scientific Methodology course at PUCRS (2026). 📄 **The paper is written in Portuguese** (*Inteligência Artificial na Computação Forense: aplicações, desafios e explicabilidade na análise de evidências digitais*); this page summarizes it in English.

📎 **Read the paper (PDF, in Portuguese):** [Artigo_Metodologia_Cientifica_redigido.pdf](Artigo_Metodologia_Cientifica_redigido.pdf)

## Abstract

Digital forensics faces the challenge of analyzing ever larger and more diverse volumes of digital evidence, which has motivated the use of artificial intelligence (AI) techniques in the field. This article analyzes how AI has been applied to digital forensics and which challenges must be addressed for its results to be reliable and understandable.

An exploratory literature review was carried out on Google Scholar, resulting in three studies published between 2021 and 2024. The comparative analysis showed that AI is applied mainly in the triage, examination and analysis phases, with benefits such as shorter investigation time and the ability to process large volumes of data. On the other hand, the reliability of results is still limited by data quality, generalization, false positives and negatives, and lack of validation on real cases. Explainability (XAI) emerges as a proposal to make results understandable, especially in court, but its admissibility cannot yet be determined.

The article concludes that AI should be seen as a support tool for the human expert, not as a replacement.

**Keywords:** artificial intelligence, digital forensics, digital evidence, machine learning, explainability

## Research question

> How has artificial intelligence been applied to digital forensics, and which challenges must be addressed for its results to be reliable and understandable?

## Method

- Exploratory literature review on Google Scholar, using four search strategies (AI, machine learning, reliability and explainable AI, each combined with "digital forensics").
- Selection prioritized studies from 2021 onward that focus directly on AI applied to digital forensics.
- The analysis is organized by themes instead of study by study: applications along the forensic process, benefits, limits of reliability, explainability, the role of the human expert, and open gaps.
- Limitations are stated in the paper: a small number of studies, a single database and a single language.

| Study | Year | Type | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jarrett & Choo | 2021 | Literature review | Automation and AI |
| Hall, Sakzad & Choo | 2022 | Opinion paper with a theoretical model | Explainable AI (XAI) |
| Dunsin et al. | 2024 | Literature review (described as systematic) | AI/ML in digital forensics and incident response |

## Main findings

- **Where AI is used:** mostly in triage, examination and analysis, for tasks like malware and intrusion detection, file classification and clustering of large volumes of data.
- **Benefits:** shorter investigation time and the ability to handle large volumes of evidence. Cost reduction is stressed mainly by one of the three studies.
- **Limits of reliability:** data quality and bias, difficulty generalizing across devices and formats, false positives and negatives, adversarial attacks, and a lack of validation on real cases.
- **Explainability:** the need for explanations grows as evidence gets closer to the presentation phase, that is, to court. Whether XAI-based evidence would be admissible is still an open question.
- **The human expert stays:** their role shifts from tool operator to validator and interpreter of the results.
